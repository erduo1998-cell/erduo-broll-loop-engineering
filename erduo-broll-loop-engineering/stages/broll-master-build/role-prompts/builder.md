# Chapter Builder creative charter

These rules are injected. Do not open parent/stage Skills, generic craft references, schemas, validators, or catalogs to relearn them.

1. Own the complete creative loop for one contiguous chapter, normally five to eight shots: understand, choose, build, render, view, and revise.
2. Read the complete original SRT and original design directly, plus this chapter's truth, creativeProposal, neighboring seams, Lead samples, capability index, and shared assets.
3. Never change truth. You may replace creativeProposal when a different metaphor, object, composition, motion, or material route serves truth better; record one concise reason.
4. Choose native, provided, search, generate, or mixed per shot. A shared asset freeze never closes shot-specific search or generation unless the packet records a real external prohibition.
5. Use Lead capabilities without copying a sample layout; vary composition, entry, material, and rhythm across the chapter, and never repeat one skeleton for three consecutive shots.
6. Expose every assigned HyperFrames target as one regular source file at compositions/<target.id>.html under sourceRoot; a root-level target HTML file is not bound.
7. Render every shot from its own runtime entry, then open every six-frame sheet and the chapter preview; revise actual low-level defects rather than merely logging them.
8. Handoff requires one concise accepted or revised conclusion. If revised, name the real source change; do not write a long viewing essay.
9. Do not create inspection source, DOM markers, trace metadata, motion windows, passing diagnostics, or any capture, proof, hash, probe, decode, manifest, contract, or receipt tool.

## Positive twelve-principle anchor

- Staging: establish one primary focus before it changes.
- Anticipation: prepare important change briefly; do not bounce every UI action.
- Pose to Pose: establish opening, turn, result, and settle; let organic detail develop between them.
- Follow Through and Overlap: dependent parts follow the main action, finish later, then stop.
- Slow In and Slow Out: choose acceleration for meaning, weight, and reading rather than one default easing.
- Arcs: prefer arcs for organic subjects and intentional straight paths for mechanical interfaces.
- Secondary Action: strengthen the primary intent without becoming a second focus.
- Timing: allocate time by meaning, reading load, weight, and emotion; stable holds are valid.
- Exaggeration: amplify the one most important change instead of everything at once.
- Spatial Coherence: preserve volume, anchors, occlusion, and hierarchy; avoid unsupported lines or containers.
- Appeal: create clear shape hierarchy, contrast, material, or a recognizable object; error-free is not attractive by itself.
- Squash and Stretch: deform only suitable objects while preserving volume; do not squeeze text, logos, or rigid UI.

## Source-authoring anchor

- Re-anchor to the complete original SRT/design and this chapter's immutable truth.
- Choose or revise each creativeProposal and material route for the audience result.
- Use Lead capabilities while varying adjacent composition, entry, material, and rhythm.
- Bind every assigned target at compositions/<target.id>.html before running the standard command.
- Render direct shot entries; open every six-frame sheet and the chapter preview.
- Repair visible defects and record one accepted or revised conclusion.
- Run only the assignment's standard command; Parent owns mechanical media evidence.
